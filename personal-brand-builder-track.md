# Personal Brand Builder Track
### Case study: Michael Harvey, Founder/CEO, EMR Inc.
### Runs alongside `brand-design-specialist-track.md` — same tool fluency, different subject.

**Why you first:** You have real, rare material to work with — an active FF/PM with CCRN, CCP-C, TSAC-F, and HSO credentials, completing a BSN, building a platform to fix a problem you've lived, with a real stage appearance (FL-CEMS "Invisible Load") and a real public launch (EMS World Expo, Booth 1731) already on the calendar. This isn't a hypothetical brief — it's a live case with real deadlines, which makes it a better proving ground than a fictional persona.

**Core distinction from company brand:** OHPAH's brand voice has one register that stays consistent everywhere. A personal brand has one *person* who has to sound recognizably like themselves across a LinkedIn post, a conference stage, and a hallway conversation at Expo — without sounding like three different people. That flex-without-fracture is the actual hard problem this track is teaching you to solve, first for yourself, before you'd ever trust an agent to do it for someone else.

---

## Module F1 — Positioning
- **Objective:** Name the specific angle you're credible on, to whom, and why — not "firefighter health advocate" (too broad) but the actual intersection you occupy.
- **Why:** Positioning is the thing every other module depends on. Get it vague and everything downstream (content, visuals, bio) will drift.
- **Exercise:** Write one sentence in the form "I'm the [specific credential/experience] who [specific claim], for [specific audience], because [specific reason no one else can say this]." Example shape, not a script for you to copy: *"I'm the active career firefighter-paramedic who built the platform I wished existed, for firefighters tired of guessing at their own health data, because I lived the problem before I coded the solution."*
- **Checkpoint:** Read your sentence to someone who doesn't know OHPAH. If they ask "wait, so what do you actually do," it's still too vague.

## Module F2 — Narrative architecture
- **Objective:** Identify your 3-4 recurring story beats — not one origin story you tell once, but a set of modular beats you can recombine depending on context (a 90-second stage intro vs. a 3-minute podcast answer vs. a written founder letter).
- **Why:** A story you can only tell one way breaks the moment the format changes. A story built from modular beats survives any format.
- **Exercise:** Write out 4 beats separately: (1) the moment the problem became personal, (2) the moment you decided to build instead of complain, (3) a specific hard moment in building EMR Inc., (4) where this goes next (OHPAH, the wellness app, the mission). Each beat: 3-4 sentences, standalone.
- **Checkpoint:** Combine beats 1+2 into a 90-second version and beats 1+2+3+4 into a 5-minute version, without rewriting from scratch — just recombining.

## Module F3 — Visual identity of the person
- **Objective:** Decide a consistent visual direction for how *you* appear — not the OHPAH product's Old World Firehouse aesthetic, but a personal visual signature (headshot style, on-camera setting, consistent color/wardrobe notes) that can complement the product brand without being identical to it.
- **Why:** A founder who visually blends into their own product's marketing becomes invisible. A founder with a distinct-but-compatible visual presence is memorable and still reads as "the person behind this."
- **Exercise:** Pull 8-10 reference images of founder/personal-brand photography you find compelling (not brand mood boards — people, portraits, stage photos) and annotate why each one works: lighting, setting, expression, wardrobe.
- **Checkpoint:** Write a one-page "personal visual brief" — the kind you'd hand a photographer before the EMS World Expo trip — covering setting, wardrobe notes, and 3 shot types you need (headshot, environmental/in-gear, candid working).

## Module F4 — Platform-specific presence
- **Objective:** Understand that LinkedIn, Instagram, and a conference stage reward fundamentally different content shapes — and decide, deliberately, which platforms you're actually going to show up on rather than spreading thin everywhere.
- **Why:** Trying to be equally present everywhere with limited time is how founder personal brands quietly die. Pick where your actual audience (firefighters, EMS leadership, first-responder health researchers, investors) actually is.
- **Exercise:** For each platform you're considering, write one line: who's there, what they want from you specifically, and what you'd post that OHPAH's brand account (@ohpah.app) wouldn't post.
- **Checkpoint:** A ranked list of 2-3 platforms you're actually committing to, with a one-line reason each — and an explicit note on which platforms you're deliberately skipping for now.

## Module F5 — Content pillars and calendar system
- **Objective:** Define 3-5 recurring themes you can post about indefinitely without running out of material — e.g., "inside the build" (founder journey), "the research" (citations, data, credibility), "the job" (firefighter life context that makes you credible), "the mission" (why this matters).
- **Why:** Without pillars, content becomes reactive and inconsistent. With them, an agent (or you) can generate on-brand ideas indefinitely.
- **Exercise:** Name your pillars, then generate 5 post ideas per pillar as a bank you can draw from.
- **Checkpoint:** You have a 20-post idea bank before you've written a single full post — proof the system generates ideas faster than you can use them.

## Module F6 — Proof and credibility asset inventory
- **Objective:** Inventory the concrete proof points that back your positioning — things that are true and verifiable, not claims.
- **Why:** Personal brand collapses into hype without proof. Yours doesn't have to, because the proof already exists.
- **Exercise:** List out: the FL-CEMS "Invisible Load" presentation, the EMS World Expo appearance, the provisional patents, the 327,498-record CAD dataset, the USF CONNECT EVOLVE accelerator relationship, your credentials (FF/PM, CCRN, CCP-C, TSAC-F, HSO), progress toward BSN/MPH. For each, write one sentence on how it supports your positioning sentence from F1.
- **Checkpoint:** A single-page "credibility sheet" you could hand to a journalist or investor without having to explain any of it verbally.

## Module F7 — Bio and asset library
- **Objective:** Write the 5-6 standard versions of "who you are" that a real personal brand needs on demand: a 15-word tag, a 50-word bio, a 150-word LinkedIn About, a spoken stage intro (someone else reading it to introduce you), a written founder-letter voice sample, and a one-line Twitter/X-style bio.
- **Why:** Without these pre-written, every time someone asks "can you send me a bio," you improvise inconsistently. With them, you paste and move on.
- **Checkpoint:** All 5-6 versions exist as a single reference document you can pull from instantly.

---

## Module F8 — Capstone: The Personal Brand Agent
*(overlaps main-curriculum Phase 5, alongside — or extending — the Module D company-brand agent)*

- **Objective:** Build an agent/harness that takes one raw idea (a sentence, a voice memo transcript, a rough thought) and outputs platform-appropriate variants that all sound like *you* — not three different people:
  1. A LinkedIn post draft
  2. An Instagram caption draft
  3. A 60-90 second spoken version (for stage or video)
  4. A one-line pull-quote version
  - All checked against your F1 positioning sentence, F2 narrative beats, and F5 content pillars, so the agent isn't just rephrasing — it's staying inside your actual system.
- **Design question worth deciding deliberately, not by default:** should this be a *separate* harness from the Module D company-brand agent, or one harness with two modes (personal vs. company) sharing the same underlying tool infrastructure (image gen, doc assembly, platform formatting)? Given how much overlaps (moodboard logic, doc assembly, platform templating), one harness with two distinct "voice profiles" loaded in is likely the more efficient build — but don't decide this until Module D exists and you can see what's actually shared.
- **Checkpoint:** Feed the agent one rough idea you haven't pre-written, and get back a LinkedIn post and a stage-version paragraph that both clearly sound like you, not generic founder-content voice.

## Practical near-term application
You have two real, dated opportunities to test this before it's even fully built:
- **FL-CEMS presentation ("Invisible Load")** — a real stage appearance. Even a partial F1-F3 pass (positioning + narrative beats + a visual brief for photos at the event) gives you something concretely useful before you get there.
- **EMS World Expo (Sept 28-Oct 2, Booth 1731)** — your public launch. This is the natural forcing deadline for F6 (credibility sheet) and F7 (bio library) to exist in finished form, not just conceptually.

Treat these two dates as real checkpoints for this track, separate from the weekly curriculum pace — they don't wait for Week 22.
